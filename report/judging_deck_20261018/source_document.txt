AZINHACK '26 Online Round: Guidelines & FAQ
About AZINHACK '26
AZINHACK '26 is a flagship hackathon by IoSC (Intel oneAPI Student Club), GGSIPU East Delhi Campus. It runs in two stages:
1. Online round (now until 18 October 2026): build your project online and submit it.
2. 2 Day finale (21-22 October 2026): shortlisted teams participate in a 2-day format at USAR, GGSIPU East Delhi Campus, Delhi, with Day 1 consisting of build and evaluation rounds, followed by Day 2 finals for shortlisted teams, presenting to an expert jury.
Prize pool: ₹1,00,000+, plus sponsor goodies, merchandise and opportunities.
Problem statement
Open Innovation. There is no fixed problem. Pick a problem you care about, in any domain, and solve it with any tech stack or approach.
The one mandatory rule: TinyFish
TinyFish integration is mandatory. Your project must use TinyFish in a way that is functional and relevant to what you are building. A token mention does not count. You decide how it fits into your product, and you must show and explain it in your submission.
TinyFish helps AI apps and agents work with the live web. You can explore:
* Search API: get fresh information from the live web
* Fetch API: pull structured, AI-ready content from webpages
* Web Agent: run goal-driven, multi-step tasks on websites
* Browser Infrastructure: control cloud browsers with tools like Playwright and CDP
Great fits include AI agents, research tools, automation systems, data products and other web-connected apps.
How the online round works
1. Register on Unstop and form your team.
2. Build your project online. Pick a problem, build a solution and integrate TinyFish.
3. Submit your project by 18 October 2026,(see the checklist below).
4. Screening: submissions go through a technical and originality check and are scored using the marking below.
5. Shortlisting: shortlisted teams are invited to the 2 Day finale.

About the social media post (10 points)
Post about your project on any platform (LinkedIn, X or similar). This helps us evaluate faster, because your post shows how you present and explain your work.
* Keep the post focused on your project: what it does, who it helps and what makes it cool.
* You do not have to mention the hackathon in the post. It is about your project.
* Add a short demo video, screenshots or a GIF if you can. It makes the post much stronger.
* Include a link to your GitHub repo.
* Make sure the post is public so the jury can open it.
Submission checklist
* A public GitHub repo with working code and a README that explains the project, how to run it and how TinyFish is used
* A demo video [add length, e.g. 2-3 minutes] showing the project working, including your TinyFish integration
* The link to your public social media post
* Submit everything through Unstop before the deadline
Ground rules
* Be original. Projects go through a technical and originality screening.
* TinyFish must be part of your project, or it is not eligible for the TinyFish points.
* Make sure your repo and post links are public and working. We cannot score what we cannot open.
* The organizers' and jury's decisions are final.
What happens at the finale
Shortlisted teams come to the USAR campus on 21-22 October for the 2 day finale. Day 1 consists of build and evaluation rounds, followed by Day 2 finals for shortlisted teams, presenting before the jury, and networking with industry and the community. Full finale details will be shared with shortlisted teams.
FAQ
Do I need to come to the campus for the online round?
No. The online round is fully online. Only shortlisted teams attend the finale at USAR.
Do I have to use TinyFish?
Yes. It is the only mandatory requirement. Everything else about your project is up to you.
What should I build?
Anything. This is an Open Innovation track. Choose a real problem and solve it your way.
How do I get started with TinyFish?
Sign up through the TinyFish link below to claim $13 in free credits, then explore the Search, Fetch, Web Agent and Browser Infrastructure tools.
Does the social media post have to mention AZINHACK?
No. Just focus on your project. It is there to help the jury see how you present your work.
Is a GitHub repo compulsory?
Yes. A working GitHub project is worth 20 points, so make sure your repo is public and your code actually runs.
What if my TinyFish integration is very small?
It needs to be meaningful and relevant to your use case. A token or decorative use will score poorly.
What happens after I submit?
Your project is screened and scored. Shortlisted teams get an invite to the 2 day finale on 21-22 October.
What is the team size, and can I work solo?
Team Size : 1-4
